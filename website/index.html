Keep the things you paste every week.

History holds text, links, images, files and colours, each with the app it came from, for a day, a year or forever. Pinboards keep the rest: addresses, replies, colours, commands. Give each one a name and a colour. Anything on a pinboard stays when old history is cleared.

Pick a pinboard to show only its items in the strip.

Type as soon as the strip opens. Words match titles and content; filters narrow it down to an app, a type, a date, a device or a pinboard.

⇧⌘C start a Paste Stack

Paste a list, in order.

Press ⇧⌘C and copy a few things in a row. Each ⌘V then pastes the next one in the order you copied them, so a form or an address takes three keystrokes instead of three trips back and forth.

    Private by default.

    Your history lives in a database on your Mac. There’s no account, no analytics and no telemetry, and the project runs no servers.

    Nothing you copy in a password manager is saved, and neither is anything an app marks as concealed or temporary. Add your own apps to ignore, or pause capturing for 15 minutes up to 8 hours.

    On until you switch them off

    Updates
    One anonymous request to GitHub a minute after launch and every six hours after. A new version is checked against its published checksum and signature, then installed in the background.
    Link previews
    When you copy a link, Super Paste fetches the page’s title and icon, the same request your browser makes to open it.

    Off until you switch them on

    Folder sync
    Keeps your Macs in step through a folder they share, in iCloud Drive, Dropbox or anywhere else. Everything is encrypted on your Mac before it’s written there.
    MCP server
    Lets assistant apps you approve search your history, on this Mac only. It listens on 127.0.0.1 and turns away web pages and other computers.

    Install it in one line.

    Paste this into Terminal. It downloads the latest release, moves it to Applications and opens it.

    curl -fsSL https://udsy19.github.io/super-paste/install.sh | bash
    Download the DMG Universal: Apple silicon and Intel

    Super Paste isn’t notarized by Apple, so macOS stops the first launch of a downloaded copy. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You do this once, and the one-line installer skips it.

    On first launch, allow Accessibility when Super Paste asks, so it can paste straight into the app you’re in. Without it, items go to the clipboard and you press ⌘V yourself.

    Requires macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Super Paste keeps itself up to date; switch that off in Settings → About. To remove it, run the same command with -s -- --uninstall after bash.
