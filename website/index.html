Everything you copy, as a card.

Each copy becomes a card with the app it came from across the top, the content below, and the details a glance needs: character count, link title and domain, pixel size, file path, hex code. The same thing copied twice moves to the front instead of piling up.

History is kept forever by default; shorten it in Settings → General (a day to a year). Pinned items are never deleted. Rich text keeps its formatting and pastes as plain text when you hold ⇧.

app:Safari type:link date:today

Find anything by typing a few letters.

Type as soon as the strip opens. Words match titles and content by word prefix, ignoring case and accents. Add a filter to narrow by app, type, date, device, pinboard or title. Filters of the same kind OR together; different kinds AND together.

⌘F opens a filter browser with the apps, dates, devices, pinboards and types in your history, so you never have to remember the syntax.

⌘← ⌘→ switch pinboards

Pinboards for the things you paste every week.

Addresses, replies, commands, the colours of a brand. Pin an item to a pinboard from its context menu or by dragging it onto the tab. Pinned items keep their place when old history is cleared, and the card header takes the pinboard's colour so you can tell at a glance where something lives.

  • Eight colours, any nameRight-click a tab to rename, recolour or delete it. Drag cards to put a pinboard in the order you want.
  • In several pinboards at onceOne item can be pinned in more than one place; unpinning never deletes it.
  • Searchable like everything elsepinboard:Work invoice finds it; the filter browser lists every pinboard.
  • Pinned-only itemsWrite something with ⌘N straight into a pinboard: it never has to pass through the clipboard.
⇧⌘C start a Paste Stack

Copy three things. Paste them one, two, three.

A Paste Stack turns ⌘V into a queue. Start one, copy several things in a row, then go to where they belong and press ⌘V once per item: each press pastes the next one, in the order you copied them.

It's for the moments a clipboard holds too little: moving a name, an email and an address from a spreadsheet into a form; pulling five quotes out of a PDF into your notes; copying values from one app into labelled fields in another without flipping back and forth each time.

  1. Press ⇧⌘CThe Paste Stack panel appears at the top right, empty.
  2. Copy the name, the email, the addressEach copy lands in the stack as a row.
  3. Click into the form, press ⌘VThe first item is pasted and leaves the stack.
  4. ⌘V again, and againSecond item, third item. ⇥ between fields as usual.
  5. Press ⇧⌘C to close it⌘V is a normal paste again. The arrow in the panel flips the order (top or bottom first).

    The strip is a non-activating panel: the app you came from keeps focus, so a paste goes exactly where your cursor was. Pick a card and press Return, double-click it, or hit its number.

    • Return or double-clickPastes the selected cards and closes the strip. Several selected (⇧-click, ⌘-click, ⌘A) paste one after another.

    no account · no analytics · no servers

    Private by default. Local by design.

    Your history is a SQLite database in ~/Library/Application Support/Super Paste. Super Paste has no account to sign in to, sends no analytics or crash reports, and the project runs no servers. The two features that can reach the network are optional and off.

    Never saved

    • Password managers. 1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane, LastPass, Enpass, Keeper, RoboForm, NordPass, Apple Passwords and Keychain Access are ignored out of the box.
    • Concealed content. Anything an app marks org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType, the convention password fields use.
    • Transient content. Auto-generated or temporary pasteboard entries.
    • Your own list. Add any app under Settings → Privacy → Ignore Applications.
    • While paused. Pause for 15 minutes to 8 hours, or until you resume (⌘T).

    Stays on your Mac

    • History and pinboards in clips.sqlite, with full-text search built in.
    • Images and files as content-addressed blobs next to it.
    • Settings in com.udsy.superpaste.plist.
    • The sync key in the macOS Keychain, on this Mac only.
    • MCP tokens stored only as hashes.

    Optional, and off

    • Folder sync (off). Writes your history, encrypted here first, to a folder you choose.
    • MCP server (off). Listens on 127.0.0.1 only, refuses web pages and other hosts, and serves only clients you approved.
    • Link previews (on). Fetches a copied link's title and icon, the request a browser would make. Settings → Privacy.
    • Automatic updates (on). One anonymous request to GitHub Releases every six hours. Settings → About.

    The full data inventory, every network call and how updates are verified: PRIVACY.md.

    Settings → Sync → Choose Folder…

    Two Macs, one history, no server in between.

    Sync works through any folder your Macs already share: iCloud Drive, Dropbox, a NAS, a USB drive you carry. Super Paste writes encrypted records and content there and reads what the other Mac wrote. Nothing is sent anywhere else, and no one running the folder service can read a word.

    1
    Turn it on and pick a folder on the first Mac. A 256-bit key is generated and kept in that Mac's Keychain.
    2
    Export the key from Settings → Sync and move the key file to the other Mac privately, for instance with AirDrop.
    3
    Import it there and choose the same folder. Both histories merge; later edits win, pinned items stay pinned.

    File names in the folder are opaque. Anyone with the folder sees ciphertext; anyone with the folder and your key sees your history, so keep the key file to yourself. Each Mac's items carry its name, so device: finds what came from where.

    Conflicts are settled the same way on every Mac: newest change wins; a deletion wins a tie with an edit. Sync state lives beside your history, never in the folder unencrypted.

    Settings → Integrations → Enable MCP

    For developers: an MCP server on localhost, and a URL scheme.

    Turn on the MCP server and the editors and assistants on your Mac can search your clipboard history, read items, save new ones and organise pinboards, once you approve each client. Eleven tools, loopback only, nothing ever written to your system clipboard.

    1 · youEnable MCPSettings → Integrations. The server starts on 127.0.0.1:39725.
    2 · clientConnectsWith the URL alone. Dynamic client registration and PKCE: no secrets to copy.
    3 · youApprove itA consent page opens in your browser naming the client. Approve or deny.
    4 · laterDisconnect any timeConnected clients are listed in Settings with when they last called. Changing the port revokes all.

    Install in one line, or drag the DMG.

    Super Paste is a 4 MB download with no dependencies. It keeps itself up to date in the background, verifying each release's SHA-256 and code signature before it swaps itself in; switch that off in Settings → About if you'd rather update by hand.

    Terminal

    Downloads the latest release, verifies the disk image, installs into Applications and opens it. Run it again to update.

    curl -fsSL https://udsy19.github.io/super-paste/install.sh | bash

    Because the installer fetches the app itself, macOS doesn't quarantine it and there's no Open Anyway step. To remove it: add -s -- --uninstall after bash. Read the script first if you like; it's 90 lines.

    DMG

    Download SuperPaste.dmg, drag Super Paste to Applications and open it. Super Paste isn't notarized by Apple, so the first launch of a browser-downloaded copy takes one extra step:

    1. Open Super Paste. macOS says it can't verify the developer. Click Done.
    2. System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security.
    3. Click Open Anyway next to "Super Paste was blocked", then Open. Only once.

    Open Anyway

    Accessibility, once

    To paste straight into other apps, Super Paste sends ⌘V to the app in front. macOS gates that behind Accessibility. Super Paste asks the first time you paste; allow it under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.

    Super Paste

    Decline and everything still works: cards are copied to the clipboard and you press ⌘V yourself. No other permission is needed. Super Paste never reads the screen or the keyboard.

    First run

    Two onboarding pages: Introduction, then Quick Start with four switches (open at login, folder sync, the activation shortcut, how long to keep history). The strip then opens on a Welcome pinboard with a few cards to try. Super Paste lives in the menu bar: no Dock icon, no main window.

    Uninstall: quit from the menu bar icon, delete Super Paste from Applications (or run the installer with --uninstall), then delete ~/Library/Application Support/Super Paste and ~/Library/Preferences/com.udsy.superpaste.plist if you want the history gone too.

    Questions

    Things people ask.

    Something missing? Open an issue and it will end up here.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. Super Paste is MIT-licensed open source with no paid tier, no trial, no account. It's built by one person and the code is on GitHub for anyone to read, build or fork.

    Why isn't it notarized?

    Notarization needs a paid Apple Developer membership. Instead of that, every release is signed with the project's own identity and published with its SHA-256 on GitHub; the app verifies both before installing an update. The one-line installer skips the Open Anyway step entirely because macOS only quarantines downloads made by a browser.

    Does it see my passwords?

    Copies made in password managers (1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane, LastPass, Enpass, Keeper, RoboForm, NordPass, Apple Passwords, Keychain Access) are never saved, and neither is anything an app marks concealed, the convention password fields follow. You can add any app to the ignore list, and pause capturing for a while.

    How long is history kept?

    Forever, unless you shorten it in Settings → General: a day, a week, a month, a year or anything in between. Pinned items are never pruned. Erase History clears the history in one go and leaves pinboards alone.

    What does the Accessibility permission do?

    It lets Super Paste send ⌘V to the app in front so a card is pasted exactly where your cursor is. That's all it's used for. Without it, choose "To clipboard" and paste yourself; everything else works the same.

    Does it work with Universal Clipboard and my iPhone?

    Copies that arrive from an iPhone or iPad through Universal Clipboard are captured like any other, with the device recorded. Super Paste itself runs on the Mac only.

    ⇧⌘V is already taken by another app.

    Super Paste tells you once and shows a warning in Settings → Shortcuts. Record a different shortcut there, or quit the other app: Super Paste keeps retrying and takes the shortcut as soon as it's free.

    How is sync encrypted?

    Each record and each piece of content is sealed on your Mac with a 256-bit key kept in the macOS Keychain, using AES-GCM, before it's written to the sync folder. File names reveal nothing. To add a second Mac you export the key from Settings → Sync and import it there; the folder service never has it.

    Does the MCP server expose my clipboard to the internet?

    No. It's off by default, binds to 127.0.0.1, rejects requests whose Host or Origin isn't local, and serves only clients you approved on the consent page. What an approved client does with what it reads (an assistant sending it to its provider, say) is up to that client; disconnect any of them in Settings → Integrations.

    Can I keep the strip from showing while I share my screen?

    Yes: turn off "Show During Screen Sharing" in Settings → Privacy and the strip is excluded from screen sharing and recordings.

    Can I change how big the cards are?

    Drag the strip's top edge: it goes from 252 to 412 points tall and the cards grow with it, from compact to large. Double-click the edge to return to the default height. There's also a slider in Settings → General.

    Where is my data, and how do I back it up?

    Everything is in ~/Library/Application Support/Super Paste: clips.sqlite for items and pinboards, Blobs for images and files. Copy the folder to back it up; Time Machine already does.

    How is it different from Paste?

    Super Paste is an independent project inspired by Paste's design, rebuilt from scratch in Swift and AppKit: the strip, the cards, pinboards and the Paste Stack behave the way you'd expect if you've used Paste. It's free and open source, keeps data local with folder sync instead of iCloud, and adds an MCP server. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Paste or its makers.

    Intel or Apple silicon?

    Both: the DMG contains a universal binary. macOS 14 Sonoma or later is required. On macOS 26 the strip uses Liquid Glass; on 14 and 15 it's a flat panel with the same layout.
